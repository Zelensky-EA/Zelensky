CLUB REFERENCE GUIDE | 2026-2027 TalonTech Club Purpose and Officer Duties TalonTech is a next-generation STEAM innovation hub where students transform scientific knowledge into useful, creative, and carefully engineered work.

Club Purpose

TalonTech exists to give students a collaborative space to explore and reinvent the natural and designed world through biology, physics, chemistry, earth science, engineering, art, and technology. Members use 3D design, digital modeling, 3D printing, laser engraving, and other fabrication tools to turn ideas into prototypes, scientific models, campus resources, and finished products. The club bridges scientific knowledge with design thinking. Members identify needs, conduct research, develop concepts, create digital designs, test prototypes, analyze failures, revise their work, and communicate final solutions. Projects may model organisms or scientific structures, solve practical campus problems, support classroom instruction, or imagine new technologies and possible futures. TalonTech also serves the Everett Alvarez High School community by completing approved 3D-printing and fabrication jobs around campus, including bathroom passes and other requested resources. The club may accept commissioned and custom work when approved by the advisor and completed in accordance with school and ASB procedures.

Core Goals

Scientific application: Apply concepts from biology, physics, chemistry, earth science, and engineering to authentic design challenges. Design and fabrication: Build skill with CAD, 3D modeling, slicing, printing, laser engraving, prototyping, and iterative improvement. Campus service: Produce safe, useful, and professional-quality items that respond to approved needs across campus. Student leadership: Give members meaningful responsibility for project planning, safety, quality, communication, finance, and team coordination. Technology growth: Expand the club’s equipment knowledge, technical capabilities, media presence, and ability to complete increasingly ambitious projects. Creative confidence: Encourage members to take thoughtful risks, learn from unsuccessful attempts, and present their ideas with clarity and pride.

Guiding Values

TalonTech is rooted in the spirit of the eagle: curiosity to investigate, precision to design and manufacture responsibly, and boldness to attempt meaningful work. Members collaborate respectfully, use equipment safely, meet commitments, welcome feedback, and represent the club professionally.

Project and Fabrication Officers

These officers manage the process that takes a request from idea to finished product. They coordinate closely so every project is feasible, safe, documented, accurately produced, and delivered at an acceptable level of quality.

Project Manager

Project intake: Clarify the requester’s purpose, specifications, quantity, deadline, approval status, and definition of a successful finished product Assignment: Work with the advisor to assign projects based on member skill, workload, equipment availability, and opportunities for members to learn Tracking: Maintain a current project queue showing ownership, status, deadlines, materials, problems, revisions, and delivery information Coordination: Connect designers, equipment operators, quality-control reviewers, and communications staff as each project moves through production Completion: Confirm that approved work is delivered, documented, and closed out with the requester

Design support: Teach and assist with CAD, 3D modeling, vector design, measurement, file preparation, and design revision File review: Check dimensions, tolerances, scale, orientation, geometry, naming, and compatibility before files enter production Problem solving: Help diagnose design failures and guide members through evidence-based revisions rather than simply replacing their work Standards: Maintain organized design files, version naming, reusable templates, and recommended practices for common TalonTech projects

Equipment and Safety Officer

2026-2027 Officer: Jacob Gomez Primary responsibility: Promote safe, organized, and responsible use of TalonTech equipment and work areas. Safety: Reinforce training requirements and approved procedures for Bambu Lab printers, the xTool laser engraver, hand tools, materials, ventilation, and cleanup Readiness: Check work areas, required protective measures, machine condition, and materials before approved production begins Maintenance: Help complete routine cleaning and maintenance, organize supplies, and report damage, unusual behavior, or needed repairs immediately Access control: Help ensure that equipment is used only by trained members with advisor permission and that unsafe work is stopped

Quality Control Officer

2026-2027 Officer: Andres Hernandez Primary responsibility: Verify that completed work meets project requirements before it is delivered or displayed. Additional Constitution Language

Articles Beyond Article 9 The following articles establish procedures specific to TalonTech’s equipment, fabrication services, digital work, public communication, leadership continuity, and responsibility to the Everett Alvarez High School community.

Article 10 — Equipment Use, Safety, and Materials

All members shall follow school safety rules, advisor instructions, equipment-specific procedures, and required training when using 3D printers, laser engravers, hand tools, computers, and other fabrication equipment. Members may operate equipment only after receiving permission and demonstrating appropriate training. The faculty advisor may immediately stop any activity considered unsafe or likely to damage equipment, materials, school property, or student work. Members shall maintain clean and organized workspaces, report damaged or malfunctioning equipment, and use materials responsibly. Deliberate misuse of equipment or repeated failure to follow safety expectations may result in the suspension or loss of equipment privileges.

Article 11 — Projects, Commissions, and Financial Procedures

TalonTech may complete approved 3D-printing, laser-engraving, design, and fabrication projects for classrooms, clubs, staff members, school programs, and other authorized clients. Projects may include commissioned or custom work when approved by the faculty advisor. Before beginning a project, the club shall consider its purpose, design requirements, material costs, equipment availability, production time, safety, and deadline. The club reserves the right to decline or modify requests that are unsafe, inappropriate, impractical, excessively costly, or inconsistent with school rules or the club’s mission. All payments, donations, fundraising proceeds, purchases, and reimbursements shall be processed according to EAHS and ASB procedures. Club funds may be used for materials, replacement parts, equipment, software, training, competitions, outreach, media, and other approved activities that support TalonTech’s mission.

Article 12 — Project Ownership and Digital Files

Designs created through TalonTech shall be stored in an organized club archive whenever appropriate. Members must respect copyright, licensing requirements, trademarks, privacy, and the intellectual property of others. The club shall not reproduce protected designs or logos without appropriate authorization. Members should receive credit for their original work. Files created specifically for an approved campus request may be retained by TalonTech so they can be repaired, reproduced, or revised in the future.

Article 13 — Quality Control

Completed products shall be reviewed before delivery. The review may include dimensions, fit, strength, function, spelling, engraving accuracy, surface quality, cleanliness, and overall appearance. Products that do not meet the approved requirements shall be revised, reproduced, or rejected. No member may deliver a product on behalf of TalonTech without approval from the project manager, quality control officer, or faculty advisor.

Article 14 — Member Conduct and Participation

Members shall treat one another, clients, school employees, equipment, and workspaces respectfully. Members are expected to communicate honestly, complete assigned responsibilities, meet reasonable deadlines, accept constructive feedback, and ask for help when needed. Discrimination, harassment, unsafe conduct, deliberate equipment misuse, theft, unauthorized production, or inappropriate designs are prohibited. The advisor may restrict participation or equipment access when necessary to protect students, equipment, or the school.

Article 15 — Media, Privacy, and Public Communication

Photographs, videos, project information, social media posts, advertisements, and public statements representing TalonTech must be approved by the communications officer and faculty advisor and ASB before publication. Members shall follow school rules regarding student privacy, media releases, campus branding, and the use of names or images. Confidential information contained in commissioned projects shall not be publicly shared.

Article 16 — Officer Vacancies and Removal

An officer who resigns, becomes ineligible, repeatedly fails to perform the duties of the position, or violates club or school expectations may be removed by a majority vote at a previously announced meeting, subject to advisor approval. A vacant position may be filled through a special election or temporary appointment approved by the membership and advisor. The outgoing officer shall transfer relevant records, files, passwords, project information, and materials to the incoming officer.

Article 17 — Records and Annual Transition

The club shall maintain organized records of meeting minutes, membership, finances, projects, designs, equipment maintenance, competitions, media, and accomplishments. At the end of each school year, officers shall prepare records and materials for the next leadership team. Digital accounts and shared files shall remain under school-approved ownership and shall not depend solely on a graduating student’s personal account.
